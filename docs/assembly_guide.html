DC35 CNet · radar badge · board rev G

Build the badge

Every part goes through a hole, so this is an evening with a soldering iron rather than a reflow oven. The order below keeps each part from getting in the way of the next.

soldering through-hole only parts 11, plus pin headers and foam tape board 148 × 220 mm, 2 layers power USB-C

Two sides, swapped

The front faces the room: the LED ring with the radar standing in it, the touch pad marked TAG, the SAO port at the tip and the copper eyes. The back faces you: the round screen, the three buttons and the ESP32 Dev Module.

A part on one side is soldered from the other. Everything on the back is soldered on the front, and the reverse. Before each part, check which side its body goes on: it is marked on every step.

Tools

  • A temperature-controlled iron and fine solder
  • Flush cutters, for trimming leads close to the board
  • A multimeter, for one check before the first power-up
  • Tweezers or a helping hand, and some tape to hold parts while you turn the board over
  • A USB-C cable and a computer with Chrome or Edge, to load the firmware

Build

  1. Check the board

    The silkscreen under DC35 RADAR SQUID on the front should read rev G, the board this guide is for.

  2. R1, the 330 Ω resistor back

    It lies flat behind the ring's DIN pad, running up and down the board. Bend both leads at 90° to fit the holes, 10.16 mm (0.4") apart, push it through from the back and solder on the front. Either way round is fine. Trim the leads short on the front: the ring will sit over them.

  3. The three buttons back

    They snap into place under the screen's position. Push each one fully home so it sits flat, then solder on the front. Seen from the back they are UP, DOWN, SELECT, left to right.

  4. The piezo, BZ1 front

    Below the ring on the left. The + lead goes in the square pad, marked "+". That side is GPIO32. A piezo driven like this works either way round, so this is about tidiness, but it costs nothing to get right. Solder on the back.

    Check the partIt must be a piezo transducer, which the ESP32 drives directly. A magnetic "passive buzzer" is a coil and needs a transistor; fitted here it will barely make a sound.

  5. The SAO header, J3 front

    At the tip of the mantle, on the same side as the ring. The header's short ends go through the board and its long pins point straight out from the front, so an add-on plugs in facing away from the badge. Solder one pin on the back, check the header stands square to the board, then solder the rest. Pin 1 (3V3) is top left, pin 2 (GND) below it.

    The SAOs that plug in here have their own guide: Build the SAOs.

  6. The round display, DS1 back

    The screen goes behind the ring, its 7 pins through the board below it. It solders straight to the board, with no socket. Solder one pin on the front and check that the screen sits square and centred in its outline before doing the other six.

    Then trim those pins flush on the front. The ring sits over them in step 8, and long tails would hold it off the board or short against it.

  7. The ESP32 Dev Module, U1 back

    Across the head of the squid. Turn it so the USB-C socket is at the board edge, flush with it, and the antenna end lies over the bare, copper-free area at the other edge. Wrong way round, USB won't reach and Wi-Fi will be weak.

    It solders straight to the board, with no sockets. Tack two opposite corner pins, check it sits flat and square to its outline, then solder all 30 on the front and trim the tails.

  8. The LED ring, RING1 front

    The ring stands on six individual standard 2.54 mm pin headers, one in each of its holes. Push each pin through the board from the front, plastic spacer down, and the ring onto the pins above. The spacers lift the ring just enough to clear the trimmed display pins underneath.

    • Solder the pins on the back first, keeping them upright, then sit the ring on them and solder each pin to the ring from above. Trim the pins flush with the top of the ring.
    • Line LED 0 up with the "LED 0 ▼" mark at 12 o'clock. LED 0 is the first LED after the ring's data input.
    • Solder DIN, both 5V and both GND. DOUT is only there to hold the ring; solder it for strength.
    CheckBefore the radar goes in, look across the ring's inner edge: nothing from the display should touch it.

  9. The radar pigtail, J2 front

    Four pads beside the ring, inside its opening, labelled 5V, GND, TX, RX in the radar's own order. Solder the pigtail's four wires in, then solder on the back.

    Wire by the labels, not the coloursPigtail wire colours vary from batch to batch. Plug the pigtail into the radar first and follow each wire from its pin to the matching label.

    Keep the wires low and run them under where the radar will sit: anything standing up near the radar's face is in its field of view.

  10. The radar, U2 front

    Plug the pigtail in, then stick the radar down in the middle of the ring with foam tape at least 3.5 mm thick:

    • Antenna face out, toward the room. The radar can't see through the board.
    • Long side vertical, JST connector at the top. That way round, left and right come out correct.

    The JST connector stands about 3.2 mm off the back of the module, so thinner tape leaves the radar rocking on it. The seven pads along the radar's edge are test pads: nothing solders to them.

  11. Check before power

    • Look over every joint, especially the two rows of Dev Module pins and the display's seven, for bridges between neighbours.
    • With the meter on continuity, touch the 5V and GND pads of J2. No beep. A beep is a short: find it before plugging in.

  12. Load the firmware

    Plug the Dev Module into a computer and open Flash from your browser in Chrome or Edge. It checks the release's signature, then installs it. A blank badge has nothing to keep, so it doesn't matter whether "Erase device" is ticked.

    No Chrome or Edge? The cable kit does the same from a command line. If the computer doesn't see the badge, the page explains the USB driver.

  13. First power-up

    On a power bank or a good USB charger, the badge shows its splash screen and then the menu. Then check each part:

    • Screen: clean picture. Speckled or garbled? Unplug, hold UP and DOWN and plug in again: that sets a slower, safe screen speed.
    • Buttons: UP and DOWN move through the menu, SELECT opens an entry. BOOT, the small button on the Dev Module, goes back.
    • Ring: one dim dot creeps round in the menu. In Bling mode the whole ring lights.
    • Radar: the screen says NO RADAR - DEMO if it hears nothing from the radar; check the pigtail and its plug. If it's working, Bling mode on its FOLLOW pattern follows you as you move.
    • Sound: the menu clicks when you move through it, with Settings → Sound on ALL.
    • Touch pad: in Games → Sneak, a slap on TAG starts a round.
    ThenSet the badge's name and Wi-Fi from Settings, and it keeps itself up to date from then on. The badge guide covers every mode.

  14. Mount the power bank

    The panel behind the arms is the size of a slim 91 × 63 mm power bank. Stick the bank to the panel's back with double-sided foam tape, its edges lined up with the panel's, and run a short USB-C cable to the Dev Module. From the front, the arms now hang over the printed reef instead of the bank.

If something's wrong

You seeLikely cause
NO RADAR - DEMOThe radar isn't talking. Check the pigtail is pushed fully into the radar, and that TX and RX went to the pads labelled TX and RX, not by colour. Swapped TX/RX is the usual one.
The radar tracks you the wrong way, left for rightIt's mounted upside down. Turn it so the JST connector is at the top.
The ring stays dark, or shows wrong coloursCheck R1 and the DIN, 5V and GND joints on the ring. On a weak USB supply the ring can glitch: try a better power bank.
The animation on the ring is turnedLED 0 isn't at the "LED 0 ▼" mark. Turning the ring is the only fix.
Wi-Fi won't connect, or dropsUsually power: a laptop port through a long cable sags under Wi-Fi. Use a good charger or power bank. Also check the Dev Module's antenna end is over the bare area.
No soundSettings → Sound is OFF, or the part is a magnetic buzzer rather than a piezo.
The computer doesn't see the badgeA charge-only USB cable, or the USB driver: see No badge in the list.
