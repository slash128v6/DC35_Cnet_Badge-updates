Sneak

Red Light, Green Light, refereed by a 24 GHz radar. One badge, any number of players, and nobody else needs a badge at all.

menu Sneak radio none field 6 m, ±60° sound optional firmware 1.0.44 board rev F as of 23 Nov 2026
The idea

Don't get seen moving

One badge stands on a table and faces the room. It is the referee. Everyone else starts at the far side and tries to reach it and slap TAG.

Most of the time the referee has its back turned, and you can move. Then it warns you, turns round and looks. While it's looking, the radar sees anyone moving, works out where they are, and the ring points straight at them. Get pointed at and you walk back to the start.

Sneak needs exactly one badge. If there are two in the room, Duel is the other game — see the Duel rulebook.

Before the first round

Setting up

  1. Stand the badge upright, facing the players.

    The radar sees 60° either side of straight ahead, and that wide axis has to stay horizontal — so the badge stands up the way it hangs, not lying flat and not tipped on its side. Nothing in front of it: a laptop screen or a water bottle is a wall to a 24 GHz radar.

  2. Mark a start line up to 6 m away, inside the wedge.

    Anyone further than 6 m is ignored, so start there or closer. Players must approach from the front. Someone walking round the side, outside the radar's view, can't be caught, so the house rule is: stay in front of it.

  3. Pick Games → Sneak.

    The screen says ready and the ring breathes white.

  4. Press SELECT to start the round.

    Whoever is running the badge presses it, then walks out of the field or stands still behind it.

One round

Blind, turn, look, repeat

Widths show typical phase length. Blind and Look are a different random length every time, so nobody can count down to the turn. The badge goes Blind → Turning → Look over and over until somebody tags it.

A low double buzz, the ring pulses red, and a white light points at each player who moved. They walk back to the start. Then the referee goes blind again for everyone, which gives them time to walk back without being caught twice.

Tagged · 5 s

Someone reached the badge

Slap TAG and the round is yours: a rising fanfare, the ring goes rainbow and the screen says TAGGED! After 5 s it's back to Ready, and SELECT starts the next round.

The fine print

What counts as caught

TAG is the whole front of the badge. The mantle — the wide top of the squid, above the ring — is a touch pad, marked with a bullseye and the word TAG, and touching it tags exactly as SELECT does. It is the button to go for at a run: you can hit it with a flat hand without aiming, and without groping round the back for a 6 mm tactile switch. SELECT still works for anyone who prefers it.

You get 0.4 seconds to stop. The first 0.4 s of Look doesn't count, so a player who stopped on the amber warning isn't caught while they settle.

A flicker isn't a catch. The radar has to see you moving for 0.15 seconds before it calls it. It feels speed as well as position, so a brisk step is caught the moment it starts, not once you've arrived.

You have about 20 cm of slack. That is the threshold the badge calls movement: breathing and a little sway sit under it, a step does not. Plant your feet, keep your arms by your sides and let it be the other players who fidget.

Freezing mid-step doesn't save you. Stand perfectly still and the radar loses sight of you, which is the point. But step past the 20 cm and then freeze, and you're caught all the same.

You can tag at any moment, Look included. If you're close enough to slap it, slap it: a tag wins even under a red ring.

Three at a time. The radar tracks up to three people at once. In a bigger group it may only catch three movers in one look, which is luck, not a rule.

What the screen shows

The referee's view

The screen is round, 240 px across, and faces whoever is running the badge — not the players. The radar's fan opens upward from the bottom of the circle: the wedge is its ±60° view, the arcs are 1.5 m apart out to 6 m, and the ring of dots around the rim mirrors the 24 LEDs on the front, so you can see what the room sees.

Across the room

Read the ring, not the screen

The players can't see the screen, so the 24-LED ring on the front tells them everything they need.

  • ReadySlow white breathing. Waiting for SELECT.
  • BlindGreen, with a bright spot chasing round. Go.
  • TurningFast amber flashing. Stop now — nobody can say they didn't see it coming.
  • LookSolid red, no animation. It's watching, and it holds still too.
  • CaughtPulsing red, with a steady white light pointing at each player who moved.
  • TaggedRainbow. Someone reached the badge.

How the pointer works

Read the ring like a compass facing the room: 12 o'clock is straight ahead of the badge. A white light on the badge's left means the player on the left, as you see it standing in front of it, so it reads correctly from anywhere in the room.

Angles are stretched one and a half times so that players standing close together get separate lights. Two players within about 10° of each other share one light: sort it out between you, or both go back.

With a piezo fitted

Listen, too

The ring is there to be watched, but a player creeping in is watching their own feet. So the referee also calls the game out loud: the warning before it turns is a sound you can't miss while looking at the floor.

Blind starts
One high beep. Go. You hear it at the start of every blind phase, including the one after a catch.
Turning
Two falling notes. Stop now.
Look
Nothing. Silence is the referee watching.
Caught
A low double buzz. Somebody is walking back.
Tagged
A rising four-note fanfare.

Sound needs a piezo on the badge. Rev F boards don't have a place for one, so it is added by hand on the Dev Module's D32 pin; later boards have it built in. Without one the game is exactly the same, just silent. Settings → Sound turns it off, for a game in a room that needs to stay quiet.

Controls

Buttons and the pad

The three buttons are on the back, under the screen: UP, DOWN, SELECT from left to right as you look at the screen. The touch pad is on the front, across the mantle.

Try it with nothing connected

Demo mode

If no radar is heard within 3 s of switching on, the badge shows NO RADAR - DEMO and plays against itself: simulated players creep in while it's blind, some get caught, and every so often one of them tags it. New rounds start by themselves 2.5 s after Ready.

Demo runs the real referee on simulated players rather than faking the screen, so what you see is exactly how the game scores. It's a one-time decision at startup: plugging a radar in later needs a restart.

Honest status

Still being tuned

What hasn't been settled yet, most important first.

  • How still is still enough

    The 20 cm threshold comes from bench measurement — enough to ignore breathing and sway, small enough to catch a step — and it holds up in the two-badge Duel game, which is built on the same number. It has not been through a room full of players competitively trying to creep, which is the test that matters.

  • Phase lengths are a first guess

    In demo, simulated players cross the room in a single blind phase. Real games will probably want shorter blind phases, and they haven't been played at scale yet.

  • No score

    The screen counts rounds and catches, but there are no points and no winner across rounds. Keep score out loud.

  • Two radars in one room

    What a dozen badges in one space do to each other's radar hasn't been measured. Two have been run facing each other without trouble, which is encouraging rather than conclusive.
