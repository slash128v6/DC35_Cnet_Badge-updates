Build the SAOs

Four 30 mm add-ons that light up a shape, each with three parts. A quarter of an hour each, and the badge can tell which one you plugged in.

soldering through-hole only parts 3 per SAO boards 4 per panel, 30 mm round power from the badge, 3.3 V

The set

A circle, a triangle, a star and an umbrella, each the outline of a reef creature printed on the front: a pearl, a shark fin, a sea star and a jellyfish. One colour-cycling LED behind the board lights the outline, which is bare board, so the light comes through it. Nothing to program: the LED runs itself.

The front is the printed side and faces the room. Every part goes on the back, which faces the badge, and is soldered on the front. All four boards have the same layout: only the shape, and which way the resistor is wired inside the board, differ.

Tools

  • A temperature-controlled iron and fine solder
  • Flush cutters, for the panel's snap-off nubs and the leads
  • Small pliers, to bend the LED's leads
  • A multimeter, for one check before plugging in
  • A built badge to plug it into

Build

  1. Snap a board out

    Each board hangs in the panel by two small tabs, on opposite corners. Hold the panel, grip the board and twist: it comes out in one go. Nip the two nubs flush with the cutters.

    Mind the printCut the nubs away from the board rather than toward it, so the cutters don't scuff the printed front.

  2. The 1 kΩ resistor back

    It lies flat along the rim on the right of the shape (on the left when you look at the back), between a snap-off tab and the LED's place. Bend both leads tight to the body, 5.08 mm (0.2") apart, push it through from the back and solder on the front. Either way round is fine. Trim the leads flush: this is the printed side.

    The resistor is the same part in the same place on all four boards. The board itself decides which pin and which supply it ties, and that is how the badge tells the shapes apart.

  3. The LED back

    At the top. It lies flat on the back with its dome pointing at the middle of the board, and its light goes into the board, which carries it round the outline.

    • The short lead, on the flat side of the rim, goes in the square pad, on the left seen from the front. The long lead takes the round pad.
    • Bend both leads 90° about 1.5 mm from the body, so it lies flat with the dome pointing inward. Push it through from the back until the body sits on the board.
    • The tip of the dome should rest on the bare outline, just over the start of the shape. Press it down so it touches the board, then solder on the front and trim.
    The right way roundBackwards, it stays dark: the LED is a diode as well as a light. Check the flat side and the short lead before soldering.

  4. The socket back

    At the bottom, lying along the rim. Push it in from the back so it sits flat and square, solder one pin on the front, check it, then the other five. Pin 1 is the square pad.

  5. Check before plugging in

    • Look over every joint for bridges, especially between the socket's six.
    • With the meter on continuity, touch the socket's pin 1 (square pad) and pin 2 next to it. No beep. A beep is a short between 3.3 V and ground: find it before plugging in.

  6. Plug it in

    The badge's SAO header is at the tip of its mantle, pins pointing out of the front. Before plugging in, note which corner of the header has the square pad. Plug the SAO on, printed side facing out, with its own square socket pad on that same corner.

    The outline lights and fades through the colours. It is brightest near the LED and softer round the far side, which is how the light travels through the board.

    Pin 1 to pin 1The other way round puts 3.3 V where ground should be. If the shape doesn't light within a second, unplug it and check.
    ThenThe badge reads each SAO's resistor and remembers every shape it has had plugged in: Desk → SAOs shows your collection. The last shape plugged in is your team, shown in Duel and on the CTF leaderboard, and having had all four captures the CTF's Shape hunt flag, so trade or borrow. On the serial console, SAO answers with the shape, the collection and the team. All of this needs badge firmware 1.0.28 or later, which badges install by themselves.

If something's wrong

You seeLikely cause
The shape doesn't lightPlugged in the wrong way round: unplug at once, and match the square pads. Otherwise the LED is in backwards, the short lead not in the square pad.
The shape is dim, or lit only near the topThe LED's dome isn't touching the board over the bare outline. Re-bend the leads so it presses down onto it.
The LED shows through the front as a bright spotIt is standing up rather than lying flat. Bend it down onto the back.
The badge names the wrong shape, or "other"Check the resistor's two joints. A dry joint reads as no resistor at all.
