Build the SAOs

Four 30 mm add-ons that light up a shape, each with three or four parts. A quarter of an hour each, and the badge can tell which one you plugged in.

soldering through-hole only parts 3 or 4 per SAO boards 4 per panel, 30 mm round power from the badge, 3.3 V

The set

A circle, a triangle, a star and an umbrella, each the outline of a reef creature printed on the front: a pearl, a shark fin, a sea star and a jellyfish. One colour LED behind the board lights the outline, which is bare board, so the light comes through it.

The badge drives the LED, so each shape lights in its own colour - the badge's colour for that team - says hello with three blinks when you plug it in, blinks white when you capture a CTF flag and breathes once you've collected all four. There's one more trick for later. On a badge that doesn't know these SAOs, it stays dark.

The front is the printed side and faces the room. Every part goes on the back, which faces the badge, and is soldered on the front. All four boards have the same layout: only the shape, which pins the parts are wired to inside the board, and the 1 kΩ (triangle and umbrella only) differ.

Tools

  • A temperature-controlled iron and fine solder
  • Flush cutters, for the panel's snap-off nubs and the leads
  • Small pliers, to bend the LED's leads
  • A multimeter, for one check before plugging in
  • A built badge to plug it into

Build

  1. Snap a board out

    Each board hangs in the panel by two small tabs, on opposite corners. Hold the panel, grip the board and twist: it comes out in one go. Nip the two nubs flush with the cutters.

    Mind the printCut the nubs away from the board rather than toward it, so the cutters don't scuff the printed front.

  2. The 1 kΩ resistor, triangle and umbrella only back

    On the circle and the star, skip this step: their lower resistor place stays empty. Brown, black, red. It takes the lower of the two resistor places on the right of the shape (on the left when you look at the back), next to a snap-off tab. Bend both leads tight to the body, 5.08 mm (0.2") apart, push it through from the back and solder on the front. Either way round is fine. Trim the leads flush: this is the printed side.

    How the badge tells the shapes apart: the LED's data pin reads as one half of a code, and this resistor, or its absence, as the other. The board decides which pins they sit on.

  3. The 330 Ω resistor back

    Orange, orange, brown. The upper resistor place, next to the LED. Every board has one. The same way: leads bent tight, through from the back, soldered on the front, either way round, trimmed flush.

    It sits in the LED's data line.

  4. The LED back

    At the upper right, seen from the front. It lies flat on the back with its dome pointing at the middle of the board, right across the bare outline, and its light goes into the board, which carries it round the shape.

    • Its four leads are, in a row: DIN, VDD, GND, DOUT. DIN goes in the square pad, the one nearest the 330 Ω resistor. DIN is the end lead on the round side of the rim, away from the flat (DOUT is the one by the flat).
    • Spread the leads to fit. They leave the LED 1.27 mm apart, and the holes are twice that, 2.54 mm, so there's room to solder without bridging. Fan them out in their own plane over the first 3 mm from the body: by there the two outer ones are about 2 mm further out each side, the two middle ones just over half a millimetre. Hold the LED over the holes to check.
    • Then bend all four together 90° at that point, 3 mm from the body, so it lies flat with the dome pointing inward. Push it through from the back until the body sits on the board.
    • Press the body down so it touches the bare outline, then solder on the front and trim.
    The right way roundBackwards puts 3.3 V on its ground lead, which can kill it. Check that DIN, the round-side lead, is in the square pad before soldering.

  5. The socket back

    At the bottom, lying along the rim. Push it in from the back so it sits flat and square, solder one pin on the front, check it, then the other five. Pin 1 is the square pad.

  6. Check before plugging in

    • Look over every joint for bridges, especially between the socket's six.
    • With the meter on continuity, touch the socket's pin 1 (square pad) and pin 2 next to it. No beep. A beep is a short between 3.3 V and ground: find it before plugging in.

  7. Plug it in

    The badge's SAO header is at the tip of its mantle, pins pointing out of the front. Before plugging in, note which corner of the header has the square pad. Plug the SAO on, printed side facing out, with its own square socket pad on that same corner.

    It blinks three times and the outline lights in the shape's colour: cyan for the circle, red for the triangle, amber for the star, magenta for the umbrella. It is brightest near the LED and softer round the far side, which is how the light travels through the board.

    Pin 1 to pin 1The other way round puts 3.3 V where ground should be. If the shape doesn't light within a few seconds, unplug it and check.
    ThenThe badge reads each SAO's resistor and remembers every shape it has had plugged in: Desk → SAOs shows your collection. The last shape plugged in is your team, shown in Duel and on the CTF leaderboard, and having had all four captures the CTF's SAO set flag - and the four together open The Deep, the CTF's text adventure. On the serial console, SAO answers with the shape, the collection and the team. SAOLED BLINK blinks its LED. The shapes need badge firmware 1.0.28 or later and the LED 1.0.45 or later, which badges install by themselves.

If something's wrong

You seeLikely cause
The shape doesn't lightPlugged in the wrong way round: unplug at once, and match the square pads. Then check the 330 Ω's joints and the LED's, and that the LED's DIN is in the square pad.
It lights on this badge but not on someone else'sAs it should: the LED needs a badge that tells it what to show.
The shape is dim, or lit only near the topThe LED isn't touching the board over the bare outline. Re-bend the leads so it presses down onto it.
The LED shows through the front as a bright spotIt is standing up rather than lying flat. Bend it down onto the back.
The badge names the wrong shape, "other", or nothing at allThe LED is half of the shape's code, so check the 330 Ω's and the LED's joints as well as the 1 kΩ's. A dry joint reads as no part at all. A circle or star with a 1 kΩ fitted reads wrong: take it out.
SAO set: four 30 mm boards on one 80 mm panel, UV-printed front, ENIG. Each board: one LED, one socket and a 330 Ω for the LED; the triangle and umbrella add a 1 kΩ ID resistor. See also the badge's assembly guide.
